Living Fully Part II: Single Living — The Difference Is a Decision

There is a small but meaningful difference between single living and living single: intentional participation. One is choosing to engage in her life, the other is quietly existing inside it. Existing is not the same as living.

Renetta Smith, Founder & CEO, no more hiding™ · October 30, 2025 ·

Original content created by no more hiding™  © 2026 no more hiding, LLC. All Rights Reserved.

INTENTIONAL PARTICIPATION breathes breath into life ... let that rest in your spirit for a moment.

This is Part II of the Living Fully series. If you missed Part I, you can begin here: Living Fully, Living Free: Single at Any Age (Part I).

Life changes pace as we age; we all feel it. The priorities that once structured our days soften: careers end, roles shift, loved ones slow down, and social circles grow smaller. Sometimes the world doesn't shrink, but our movement within it does. Right here. Right now, I want you to feel empowered to enjoy this season; to discover your happy place and actually go experience it.

Recently, I asked someone what brought her joy. She answered without hesitation: "Worshipping God." I understood the peace she meant — the comfort and steadiness. But it made me reflect on something many of us face in this season: sometimes stillness restores us, and sometimes we settle into stillness that replaces living. Faith was never meant to shrink our world — it was meant to steady us while we step back into it.

Spiritual grounding can inspire living, but it was never meant to replace living.

Peace should center you and then send you back into life again. The difference between living single and single living isn't whether you have peace — it's whether that peace becomes a place you rest… or a place you hide.

I used to be one of those women in midlife transition who were afraid to leave the house, afraid to drive at night, afraid to drive in rain, afraid that moving my body might lead to injury. It wasn't because I was incapable — it was because I had stopped practicing life. When you stop practicing, fear fills the space that courage left behind.

The antidote wasn't a big dramatic change. It was one intentional decision at a time. One dinner out — alone. One movie — alone. One road trip — alone. One golf lesson. One art class. And slowly, the fear stopped running the show.

Intentional participation is the difference between a life you're watching and a life you're living.

What Does Intentional Participation Look Like?

It doesn't have to be grand. It's the decision to show up for your own life — to stop waiting for the right moment, the right companion, the right circumstances — and to simply begin.

  • It's making a dinner reservation for one and enjoying every bite.
  • It's signing up for the class you've been thinking about for two years.
  • It's saying yes to the invitation when your instinct is to stay home.
  • It's planning the trip — and actually going.
  • It's choosing joy on a Tuesday for no particular reason at all.

The goal isn't to be busy. The goal is to be present — to inhabit your own life with intention and curiosity.
