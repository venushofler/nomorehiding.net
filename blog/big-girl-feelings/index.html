Unpacking Your Big Girl Feelings: Embracing Joy, Freedom, and Self Love

Part 3 of the Living Fully, Living Free series explores what it means to embrace joy, freedom, and self-love as a single woman later in life. Discover how unpacking your feelings can open the door to living fully and authentically.

Renetta Smith, Founder & CEO, no more hiding™ · March 17, 2026 ·

Blog created 11/25/2025; Updated 3/16/2026.

Life didn't go the way we expected. Somewhere along the journey, the plans we once held so tightly slipped in a different direction. Many women reach a moment in midlife where they quietly look around and think: This isn't exactly the life I imagined.

Not necessarily worse. Just… different.

Healing, freedom, and self-discovery don't happen without a few honest conversations with yourself. But honest conversations are only the beginning. When you sit quietly with your truth, emotions often surface… grief, disappointment, anger, regret. Sometimes those emotions hurt — and they hurt deeply. And when they hurt, many of us do what women have been conditioned to do our entire lives: we compartmentalize our feelings, close the door on the conversation and move on.

Not because we don't want to grow. But because we don't want to feel the kind of pain that comes with raw emotions from unhealed hurt. And in most cases, we don't have the downtime to fully analyze and resolve hurt — that is until the hurt shows up in the form of disease, joint pain, hypertension, stroke, heart disease, and mental defect. Then we're forced to reconcile with unhealed hurt.

Emotional awareness without action keeps people stuck. Insight alone doesn't change your life. Action does.

Avoiding your feelings doesn't make the pain disappear — it only prolongs healing. Feeling your feelings is an important step toward healing, but feelings alone won't redesign your life. Sometimes the hardest action of all is simply allowing yourself to feel the truth of your situation without immediately trying to fix, manage, or escape it.

What Does Unpacking Actually Look Like?

It looks like sitting with a journal and asking yourself: What am I really feeling right now? And what is this feeling trying to tell me?

It looks like being honest about what isn't working — in your relationships, your routines, your inner narrative — without spiraling into shame.

It looks like grief. Sometimes you have to grieve the life you planned before you can embrace the life you have. Grief is not failure. Grief is how we make peace with what was so we can be present to what is.

Embracing Joy as a Practice

Joy isn't a destination. It's a practice. And like any practice, it requires intentionality — especially when the world keeps telling you that your season has passed.

Joy looks different in midlife than it did at 25. It's quieter. Steadier. Less about excitement and more about peace. It's the morning cup of coffee before the house wakes up. The book you finally have time to read. The friendship that makes you laugh until your sides hurt. The golf swing that finally lands where you aimed.

Freedom is the same. In midlife, freedom is less about going wild and more about going intentionally. It's the freedom to say no without guilt. To change your mind. To prioritize your wellbeing without apology. To build a life that reflects who you actually are — not who you were expected to be.

Self-love, in this season, is radical. It is choosing yourself — loudly, consistently, and without waiting for someone else's permission.

Self-Work Homework

Name three emotions you've been avoiding. What are they trying to tell you? What is one act of joy you can commit to this week — not someday, but this week?
