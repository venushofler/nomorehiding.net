Tell Yourself Hard Truths

We don't need more motivation — we need honesty. In this next step of the no more hiding™ self-work journey, we move beyond the uncomfortable truths we avoid and into action.

Renetta Smith, Founder & CEO, no more hiding™ · March 23, 2026 ·

Original content created by no more hiding™  © 2026 no more hiding, LLC. All Rights Reserved.

Because the life we want will never grow from the lies we keep telling ourselves.

Part of becoming the best version of yourself is first believing you deserve a life that is joyful — and then identifying what or who is blocking your happiness and wholeness.

In How to Get a Life When You've Forgotten You Had One, I introduced the NMH Self-Work Breakdown Structure™, which breaks personal transformation into a series of commitments designed to help you stop hiding and begin living fully again. The first commitment is to get brave.

I shared how I had to first acknowledge that I deserved happiness and then gave myself permission to pursue it. That required accountability. Getting brave pulls your head out of the sand. It's where you stop avoiding the hard questions and start seeking real answers — even if the answers hurt. It is the moment you stop pretending everything is fine and give yourself permission to imagine a life that feels larger, fuller, and more authentic.

NMH Self-Work Breakdown Structure™ Phase 2: Tell Yourself Hard Truths

Truth is where clarity lives.

The process of gaining clarity requires honest, sometimes uncomfortable reflection — what I like to call root-cause analysis. As you know, a large portion of my career has been spent planning, designing, building, and implementing complex projects. And the first rule of good project management is this: you cannot fix what you refuse to diagnose.

The same is true for your life.

You cannot redesign your life until you're honest about the blueprint you've been living from.

The Hard Truths We Avoid

Hard truths aren't about self-criticism. They're about clarity. Here are some of the ones I had to face on my own journey:

  • I had been waiting for circumstances to change instead of changing myself.
  • Some relationships I'd held onto had long since expired.
  • Fear had been making more decisions for me than I'd realized.
  • I had been telling myself I was "fine" when I wasn't fine at all.
  • I had confused busyness with purpose.

Telling yourself the truth isn't the same as beating yourself up. It's the opposite. It's saying: I deserve better than the story I've been telling myself, and I'm brave enough to write a new one.

Root-Cause Analysis: Going Deeper

When I was going through my self-rediscovery phase in 2023, I used several tools that led me into deep root-cause analysis. I asked myself not just what wasn't working, but why it wasn't working — and how long I had been accepting it.

The questions that helped me most:

  • What am I afraid to admit — even to myself?
  • What story am I telling myself that isn't actually true?
  • What am I waiting for permission to change?
  • What would I do differently if I wasn't afraid of what people would think?
  • What has staying comfortable cost me?
Self-Work Homework

Write down three hard truths you've been avoiding. Don't soften them. Don't qualify them. Just write what is true. Then ask: what becomes possible when I stop pretending this isn't true?
