Birdies, Bogeys, and Rolling Hills: What Every Lady Golfer Should Know Before Planning a Golf Trip to Scotland

Thinking about golfing in Scotland? Join Renetta and Erin as they share what they learned golfing in the Home of Golf — from navigating historic courses and unpredictable weather to packing the right gear, understanding golf etiquette, and building friendships along the way.

Renetta Smith and Erin Stowell · August 10, 2026 ·

Did you know that Scotland is known as "the home of golf"? Yup. While the exact origins of golf remain a topic of friendly debate among die-hard golfers, Scotland is where the game evolved into the sport we know and love today. Some say Musselburgh Links in East Lothian, one of the world's oldest golf courses still in play, is where golf originated. Others point to the Old Course at St Andrews in Fife, often regarded as the mecca of golf. And then there's Leith Links in Edinburgh, where the earliest known written rules of golf were signed by John Rattay in 1744.

Historically, golf developed largely as a gentleman's game. In 1744, a group of prominent golfers in Edinburgh organized the Company of Gentlemen Golfers — later known as the Honourable Company of Edinburgh Golfers — and established 13 written rules for a competition at Leith Links. But women were playing too. In fact, the first formal documented reference to women playing golf dates to 1738 at Bruntsfield Links in Edinburgh.

Fast-forward a few centuries, and women from around the world now travel to Scotland to experience golf in its historic home. But as a woman golfer of color, I had a question that a website or travel blog couldn't answer for me: How welcome would I actually feel playing golf in Scotland?

This post is my honest answer to that question — and everything else I wish someone had told me before I booked the trip.

What to Know Before You Go

Scotland's golf culture is steeped in tradition — which is both its greatest charm and its steepest learning curve for visitors. Here's what we learned:

The Courses

Scottish links courses are unlike anything you'll play in the United States. They're carved into coastal land, exposed to wind and weather, with undulating fairways and firm, fast greens. Don't expect manicured perfection — expect raw beauty and a genuine test of your game.

  • Book tee times well in advance — especially for iconic courses like St Andrews. Some require ballot entries months ahead.
  • Walk the course if you can. Most traditional Scottish courses are walking-only or strongly encourage it. Bring a trolley or hire a caddie.
  • Respect the pace of play. Scottish golfers take pace seriously. Know your handicap, be ready to play, and keep up with the group ahead.

The Weather

Pack for all four seasons — in a single day. Scotland's weather is famously unpredictable. We played in sunshine, wind, mist, and sideways rain all within the same round. And honestly? That's part of the magic.

  • Waterproof everything: jacket, trousers, gloves, bag cover.
  • Layers are non-negotiable.
  • Wind-resistant clothing performs better than just warm clothing.

The People

Here's my honest answer to the question I couldn't find in any travel blog: we felt welcome. Not performatively welcome — genuinely welcomed. Scottish golfers are proud of their golfing heritage and happy to share it with anyone who approaches the game with respect and curiosity.

We encountered warmth at every course, in every clubhouse, from every caddie. The golf community in Scotland is inclusive in a way that made us proud to be there — and eager to return.

We didn't just play golf in Scotland. We connected — with history, with the land, and with each other.

Etiquette Notes for Lady Golfers

  • Dress codes are enforced at most clubs — call ahead to confirm requirements.
  • Collared shirts, tailored trousers or skirts/shorts of appropriate length are standard.
  • Turn off or silence your phone on the course.
  • Repair your divots and pitch marks. Every time.
  • A friendly hello on the first tee goes a long way.

What We'd Tell Every Lady Golfer Planning This Trip

Go. Full stop. Don't wait until your game is "good enough." Don't wait for the perfect travel companion. Don't wait for the right season.

Go for the history. Go for the courses. Go for the people. Go for the stories you'll tell for the rest of your life about the round you played in the rain at a links course that's been in play for three centuries.

And go knowing that you belong on every course you step onto — in Scotland or anywhere else.

Trip Planning Resources

Visit Scotland Golf (visitscotland.com) has a comprehensive guide to courses, regions, and travel planning. For St Andrews tee times, book directly through the St Andrews Links Trust at standrews.com. And if you're traveling with a group of lady golfers, consider contacting courses directly — many offer group packages and warm welcomes for visiting women's groups.
