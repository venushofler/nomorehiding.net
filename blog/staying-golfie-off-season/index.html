10 Ways to Stay Golfie in the Off-Season

From winter golf gear to indoor simulators, keep your golf lifestyle going all winter long.

Renetta Smith, Founder & CEO, no more hiding™ · November 16, 2026 ·
golfie  ·  /ˈɡɑːl.fi/  ·  GOLF-ee

adjective · informal · golf lifestyle slang

Having the spirit, enthusiasm, and unmistakable love of golf, regardless of the season, weather, or location.

You thought I was putting my clubs away just because the clocks fell back, the leaves are falling, and the temperatures have dropped?

Puhleeeezzzzz! 😂

Honey, just because the grass has gone dormant doesn't mean your golf game has to!

My mood shifts when I can't golf. After about a week, I'm fiending to hold my driver. After about a month, I get vertigo. I can't focus, and I can't walk straight!

Okay. I'm exaggerating. Kinda.

But seriously, though. Whether you're bundling up for a chilly round, teeing off on a virtual course, or practicing your putting stroke in your living room, here are ten ways to stay golfie through the off-season:

1. Don't Put Those Clubs Away!

There's no rule that says you can't golf in the off-season. Most golf courses don't close for business when the temperature drops. Call the club to arrange a tee time as usual. Some holes may be off-limits due to winter maintenance, so it's always worth checking ahead.

2. Layer-Up, Buttercup! Winter Golf Is a Real Thing

You don't have to sacrifice style to bundle up. There's course-appropriate, fashionable winter golf attire for women, including thermal layers, golf boots — yes, ladies, golf boots are a real thing! — blister-free winter socks, golf mittens, electronic hand warmers, and heated seat cushions. Check safety precautions for any battery-powered gear.

3. Golf-Birds Migrate

Golf-lovers migrate to warmer climates during the winter months. Pack your bags, grab your clubs, and off you go to sunnier pastures! Winter golf getaways offer opportunities to explore new courses, sharpen your skills, and meet fellow golf enthusiasts. Who says you have to put your golf game on hold just because the temperatures drop back home?

4. Turn Your Living Room or Garage Into a Golf Practice Zone

Keep your golf skills sharp by practicing at home with a putting mat, alignment aids, or a simple target on a suitable floor. Transform your garage into a practice area for putting and low-impact drills. You can also explore home golf simulators designed for garages and other indoor spaces, letting you practice your swing and play virtual courses year-round. If you're practicing full swings indoors, make sure you have sufficient clearance and appropriate protective equipment.

5. Take Your Game Indoors: Commercial Golf Simulators ("Golf Sims")

The world of indoor golf simulation technology is rapidly advancing. Seriously. It's not your regular Putt-Putt. From moving floors and special effects to chirping birds, airplane flyovers, automobile traffic noise, wind, rain, and even flying sand in the bunkers, golf simulators give golfies a realistic alternative when it's unsafe to play outdoors. New golf simulator franchises are popping up worldwide. There's still a tee time, and outdoor golf etiquette still applies — just with very little walking and no cart-path-only rules!

6. Tighten Up Your Golf Wardrobe

Whether you're playing indoors or outdoors, choose properly fitted, moisture-wicking golf attire that lets you move comfortably without riding up, slipping down, or revealing more than you intended. Check that your skirts, shorts, tops, and undergarments provide adequate coverage when bending, swinging, and retrieving your ball. Follow the facility's dress code and footwear requirements, especially at business golf events. Ladies, we're here to improve our golf game — not give a whole new meaning to "I see London, I see France!"

7. Keep That Swing Swinging!

It's important to stay in shape during the off-season. Keep swinging to maintain that muscle memory. During the holidays and winter months, we tend to enjoy good food while moving a little less. So don't let those golf muscles go into hibernation. Continue practicing golf-specific mobility and strengthening exercises to maintain flexibility, balance, and proper swing mechanics. You may not be hitting the fairways every weekend, but that doesn't mean your swing has to take a winter vacation!

8. Business Golf Is Different From Social Golf

Use the off-season to refine both your professional and social skills. Golf offers opportunities to build business relationships, engage in meaningful conversations, and connect with people whose paths you might not otherwise cross. Unlike social golf, business golf places greater emphasis on strengthening professional relationships, building trust, and expanding your network in a relaxed setting. Take time to learn the rules, etiquette, terminology, appropriate attire, and pace of play so you're prepared when that invitation arrives. You don't have to be an expert golfer; you just need enough knowledge and confidence to focus on building meaningful business relationships.

A Note About Your Golf Wardrobe

While the basic dress code may be the same, business golf may call for a more polished, professional appearance than a casual round with friends. Think about the impression you want to leave with potential clients, business partners, and other professional contacts.

9. Strengthen Your Mental Game

Golf is as much a mental game as it is a physical one. Use the off-season to familiarize yourself with different golf courses, study their layouts, and think strategically about how to approach each hole. Practice staying focused, managing frustration, and adapting when a shot doesn't go as planned. Remember, the goal is to think ahead, stay confident, and keep moving forward — even when you land in the rough!

10. Give Your Golf Gear Some TLC

Give your golf equipment a little tender loving care during the off-season! Clean and polish your clubs, grips, golf shoes, and accessories, making sure everything is thoroughly dry before storing it. Inspect your clubs to see whether any need new grips, repairs, or replacement. Has your game changed, or has your body changed in ways that might call for different clubs? And when was the last time you cleaned your golf bag? Check for grass stains, worn pockets, loose stitching, and other signs of wear. If your golf shoes have removable spikes, check whether they need replacing — you can often do it yourself with the proper tool and compatible spikes, or have a golf shop do it for you. This is also a great time to assess whether your current equipment still fits your game and your needs.

The Golfie Life. Because golf is more than a season. It's a lifestyle.
